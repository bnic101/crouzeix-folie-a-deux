Folie à Deux

Two proofs of the Crouzeix conjecture appeared within a week of each other in July 2026, by two groups who did not know of each other, by two methods that turn out to be the same method. Both credit ChatGPT with the decisive step.

Numerical range

Definition. Let \(\mathcal H\) be a complex Hilbert space and \(T\in\mathcal B(\mathcal H)\) a bounded operator. The numerical range of \(T\) is \[ W(T)=\{\langle Tx,x\rangle:\ x\in\mathcal H,\ \|x\|=1\}. \]

In finite dimension \(W(T)\) is compact and convex (Toeplitz–Hausdorff); in general it is convex, and \(\overline{W(T)}\supseteq\operatorname{conv}\sigma(T)\), with equality for normal \(T\).

A \(2\times2\) matrix has an ellipse with the eigenvalues as foci. A \(3\times 3\) Jordan block has a disk. Nilpotent weighted shifts have disks. The blue dashed circle is the numerical radius \(w(T)\), the orange one is \(\|T\|\); their ratio is the Crouzeix ratio for \(p(z)=z\), and it never exceeds \(2\).

Spectral sets

Definition. A compact set \(S\subset\mathbb C\) is a \(K\)-spectral set for \(T\in\mathcal B(\mathcal H)\) if \[ \|p(T)\|\le K\sup_{z\in S}|p(z)|\qquad\text{for all } p\in\mathbb C[z]. \] For convex \(S\), functions holomorphic near \(S\) come along by Runge.

von Neumann's inequality (1951). The closed disk is a spectral set for every contraction: \[ \|T\|\le1\ \Rightarrow\ \|p(T)\|\le\sup_{\mathbb D}|p|. \] Also the closed right half-plane, when \(\operatorname{Re}T\ge0\).

Two proofs, and they are the two themes of this talk.

  • Function theory. von Neumann: reduce to Blaschke products, check Möbius maps by hand.
  • Dilation theory. Sz.-Nagy: \(T\) is the compression of a unitary \(U\), and \(p(T)=P_{\mathcal H}\,p(U)|_{\mathcal H}\).

Crouzeix's conjecture

Conjecture (Crouzeix 2004). Let \(T\) be a bounded operator on a Hilbert space \(\mathcal H\). Then \(\overline{W(T)}\) is a \(2\)-spectral set for \(T\).

Remark. The constant is sharp. For \(N=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\0&0\end{pmatrix}\) and \(p(z)=z\), \[ W(N)=D(0,\tfrac12),\qquad \|p(N)\|=1=2\cdot\tfrac12=2\sup_{D(0,1/2)}|p|. \]

Antecedents.

  • von Neumann: constant \(1\), only for contractions.
  • Berger 1965, Berger–Stampfli 1967, Okubo–Ando 1975: the disk is a \(2\)-spectral set whenever it contains \(W(T)\). Via \(2\)-dilations, \(T^n=2V^*U^nV\).

So Crouzeix asks for the disk's constant on every convex set. For \(2\times2\) matrices he proved it, with equality only when \(W(T)\) is a disk.

The numerical range of the nilpotent has radius ½ while the norm is 1.

Technique, 1999 to 2026: one object, the double-layer potential. Shrinking \(\Omega\) to \(\overline{W(T)}\), \(\sup_\Omega C_\Omega\le2\) is the conjecture.

Double-layer potential

For \(\sigma\in\partial\Omega\) let \(\nu=\nu(\sigma)\) be the outward unit normal and \(ds\) arclength. For \(z\in\Omega\), \[ \mu(\sigma,z)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\Big(\underbrace{\frac{\nu}{\sigma-z}}_{\text{first layer}}+\underbrace{\frac{\bar\nu}{\bar\sigma-\bar z}}_{\text{second layer}}\Big)=\frac1\pi\operatorname{Re}\frac{\nu}{\sigma-z}. \]

  • First layer: the Cauchy kernel, \(\frac{1}{2\pi}\frac{\nu\,ds}{\sigma-z}=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\frac{d\sigma}{\sigma-z}\).
  • \(\mu\,ds=\frac1\pi\,d\arg(\sigma-z)\): the angle at which \(z\) sees the boundary. So \(\mu\ge0\) by convexity (\(z\) is inside every supporting line), and \(\int\mu\,ds=2\) for \(z\in\Omega\), \(1\) for \(z\in\partial\Omega\).
  • Why use it? The first layer gives \(f(z)\). If we can control the second layer, we win.

Drag \(z\). The boundary is drawn thick where \(\mu\) is large. Push \(z\) to the boundary: half the mass collapses onto the nearest point, and the integral drops from 2 to 1. That is the Gauss jump.

Double-layer potential, continued

Replace \(z\) by \(T\) with \(W(T)\subset\Omega\). For \(f\in C(\partial\Omega)\) put \[ C(f,T)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\partial\Omega}f(\sigma)(\sigma-T)^{-1}\,d\sigma\in\mathcal B(\mathcal H),\qquad S(f,T)=C(f,T)+C(\bar f,T)^*. \] \(S\) is the functional calculus integrated against the double-layer potential \(\mu(\sigma,T)=\tfrac1\pi\operatorname{Re}[\nu(\sigma)(\sigma-T)^{-1}]\): \[ S(f,T)=\int_{\partial\Omega}f(\sigma)\,\mu(\sigma,T)\,ds,\qquad \mu(\sigma,T)\ge0,\qquad \int_{\partial\Omega}\mu\,ds=2I. \]

Lemma (Crouzeix–Palencia 2017). For \(f\in A(\Omega)\),\quad \(\|S(f,T)\|=\|f(T)+C(\bar f,T)^*\|\le2\|f\|_{\partial\Omega}\).

Positivity and total mass \(2I\), then Cauchy–Schwarz. Positivity: \(\langle Tx,x\rangle\in\Omega\) for unit \(x\) gives \(\operatorname{Re}\bar\nu(\sigma-T)\ge0\); conjugate by the resolvent.

So the question: how does \(\|S(f,T)\|\) control \(\|f(T)\|\)? Can we estimate \(C(\bar f,T)^*\), or make it vanish altogether?

Positivity and the double-layer formula

Write \(\Phi(f)=\tfrac12S(f,T)\), a unital positive map \(C(\partial\Omega)\to\mathcal B(\mathcal H)\). For \(f\in A(\Omega)\), \[ 2\Phi(f)=f(T)+\alpha(f)(T)^*,\qquad \alpha(f)(z)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\partial\Omega}\frac{\overline{f(\sigma)}\,d\sigma}{\sigma-z}, \] where \(\alpha:A(\Omega)\to A(\Omega)\) is antilinear and \(\alpha(1)=1\). Positivity of \(\Phi\) is the lemma.

For a point \(z\): \(2\Phi(f)(z)=f(z)+\overline{\alpha(f)(z)}\), the weighted average of \(f\) on the boundary, split into a holomorphic and an anti-holomorphic piece. For the disk, \(\alpha(f)=\overline{f(0)}\), a constant.

Two ways to use the formula.

  • Estimate the companion: Crouzeix–Palencia, \(\|\alpha(f)\|\le\|f\|\), hence \(1+\sqrt2\).
  • Study the extremal \(f\), a Blaschke product: Bickel, Gorkin, Greenbaum, Overton. Not the route either 2026 proof takes.

Crouzeix–Palencia: \(1+\sqrt2\), and why not \(2\)

Let \(K=C_\Omega\), \(\|f\|\le1\), \(T=f(A)\), \(g=\alpha(f)\). Since \(\|g\|\le\|f\|\) on convex \(\Omega\), \[ TT^*TT^*=T\,\big(T+g(A)^*\big)^*\,TT^*-(fgf)(A)\,T^*, \] so \(K^4\le2K^3+K^2\) and \(K\le1+\sqrt2\). (Ransford–Schwenninger's four lines.)

Only two facts about the companion were used: \(\|f(A)+g(A)^*\|\le2\) and \(\|g\|\le\|f\|\). Under those two alone, \(1+\sqrt2\) is attained: \(T=\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\0&0\end{pmatrix}\), \(\Omega\) two small disks about \(0\) and \(1\), \(g=-f(0)\) on one and \(-f(1)\) on the other; \(h=\mp1\) gives \(\|h(T)\|=1+\sqrt2\).

Not a counterexample, since \(W(T)\not\subset\Omega\), but the end of that road. This \(g\) sends \(1\mapsto-1\). The Cauchy transform sends \(1\mapsto1\). That is the information not yet used.

Equivalent formulation

Theorem (Twisted real part). Let \(\mathcal A\) be a unital commutative operator algebra, \(\theta:\mathcal A\to\mathcal B(\mathcal H)\) a unital bounded homomorphism, and \(\alpha:\mathcal A\to\mathcal A\) a unital antilinear map such that the unital linear map \[ \theta_\alpha:\mathcal A\to\mathcal B(\mathcal H),\qquad a\mapsto\tfrac12\big(\theta(a)+\theta(\alpha(a))^*\big) \] is contractive. Then \(\|\theta\|\le2\).
  • Ransford–Schwenninger (2018) and Clouâtre–Ostermann–Ransford (2023): this statement implies Crouzeix. Take \(\theta(f)=f(T)\), \(\alpha\) the Cauchy transform; then \(\theta_\alpha=\Phi\).
  • Michael Hartz (31 July 2026) proves the theorem, by reorganising Jin's solution.

Unital and contractive implies positive: \(\operatorname{Re}a\ge0\Rightarrow\operatorname{Re}\theta_\alpha(a)\ge0\). The hypothesis is positivity of the twisted real part.

Holomorphic → resolvent transfer

Theorem (Holomorphic → resolvent transfer; Jin, operator form Hartz). Let \(T\in\mathcal B(\mathcal H)\) with \(\sigma(T)\subset\overline{\mathbb D}\). Let \(H:\mathbb D\to\mathcal B(\mathcal H)\) be holomorphic with \(H(0)=I\) and \(\operatorname{Re}H(z)\ge0\) for all \(z\in\mathbb D\). Assume that \[ \Delta(z):=H(z)-(I-zT)^{-1} \] commutes with \(T^*\) for all \(z\in\mathbb D\). Then \(\|T\|\le2\).
  • \(\Delta=0\): \(\operatorname{Re}(I-zT)^{-1}\ge0\) on \(\mathbb D\) is Berger's condition \(W(T)\subset\overline{\mathbb D}\), and the conclusion is Okubo–Ando. A commuting correction costs nothing.
  • Transfer ⇒ twisted real part (Hartz). \(T=\theta(a)\), \(c_z=(1+za)(1-za)^{-1}\), \(H(z)=\theta_\alpha(c_z)\). Then \(\operatorname{Re}H\ge0\) and \(\Delta(z)=\tfrac12(\theta(\alpha(c_z))^*-I)\), an adjoint from the commutative algebra \(\theta(\mathcal A)\ni T\).
  • Concretely \(\Delta(w)=\sum_{m\ge1}w^m\alpha(f^m)(T)^*\): the identity for every power of \(f\) at once.

Proof of the transfer theorem

On \(H^2\otimes\mathcal H\) (after scaling \(T\to rT\)), \(\operatorname{Re}M_H\ge0\) and \(M_H=M_R+M_\Delta\), \(R(z)=(I-zT)^{-1}\).

For \(W\) with \(\sigma(W)\subset\mathbb D\) let \(N_W=\{F=\sum F_nz^n:\ \sum W^nF_n=0\}\), the functions vanishing at \(W\).

  • \(\Delta(z)\) commutes with \(W\) ⇒ \(M_\Delta N_W\subset N_W\) (an ideal, as in one variable).
  • \(\Delta(0)=0\) ⇒ the reproducing kernel \(\Gamma\) of \(N_W\) at \(0\) has \(\Gamma^*M_\Delta\Gamma=0\).

Take \(W=\tfrac12T^*\): \(\ \Gamma^*(M_R+M_R^*)\Gamma=\Gamma^*(M_H+M_H^*)\Gamma\ge0\). The defect is removed exactly, not estimated.

Unwinding, with \(P=\sum4^{-n}T^{*n}T^n\), \(Q=\sum2^{-n}T^{*n}T^n\), \(Y=Q-P\ge\tfrac14T^*T\): \[ 4Y-YP-PY\ge0\ \Rightarrow\ P\le2I\ \Rightarrow\ T^*T\le T^*PT=4(P-I)\le4I. \]

The weights \(4^{-n}\), \(2^{-n}\) come from sampling at radius \(\tfrac12\). Jin's coordinates: sample the Herglotz kernel of \(H\) at \(\lambda_i/2\) and at \(0\), the origin vector chosen to cancel the diagonal correction. Same compression.

Dilation theory

Lemma (Lorist–Schwenninger). Let \(\mathcal H\) be a Hilbert space and \(T\in\mathcal B(\mathcal H)\). Suppose there exist a Hilbert space \(\mathcal K\), a contraction \(Q\in\mathcal B(\mathcal K)\) and an isometry \(V\in\mathcal B(\mathcal H,\mathcal K)\) such that the operators \[ E_n:=2V^*Q^{*n}V-T^{*n},\qquad n\in\mathbb N, \] are uniformly bounded and commute with \(T\). Then \(\|T\|\le2\).

\(E_n=0\): \(T^*\) has a \(2\)-dilation and \(\|T\|=\|2V^*QV\|\le2\) at once. The lemma tolerates an error of any size, if bounded in \(n\) and commuting with \(T\).

The dilation is Stinespring for \(\Phi\): \(\mathcal K=L^2(\partial\Omega,ds;\mathcal H)\), \(Vx=\tfrac1{\sqrt2}\mu(\cdot,T)^{1/2}x\) (an isometry because \(\int\mu=2I\)), \(Q=M_f\) with \(\|f\|_\infty=1\). Then \(V^*Q^nV=\Phi(f^n)\), and with \(T=f(A)\), \[ E_n=2\Phi(f^n)^*-f(A)^{*n}=\alpha(f^n)(A), \] which commutes with \(f(A)\) and is bounded by \(\|\theta\|\|\alpha\|\). Hence \(\|f(A)\|\le2\). The contractivity of \(\alpha\) is never used.

Proof of the lemma

\(\kappa:=\|T\|>1\) and \(x\) a unit vector with \(T^*Tx=\kappa^2x\) (ultrapower if necessary). Put \[ m_n:=\operatorname{Re}\langle E_nT^nx,x\rangle,\qquad u:=Q^*VTx-\kappa Vx. \] Two estimates on the same error vector: \[ \begin{aligned} \|u\|^2&\le 2\kappa^2-\kappa^3-\kappa m_1 &&\text{(}V\text{ isometric, }Q\text{ contractive, }n=1\text{ only)}\\ \kappa m_1&\ge-\frac{\|u\|^2}{(\kappa-1)^2} &&\text{(telescope }\kappa m_n-m_{n+1}\text{ over all }n\text{)} \end{aligned} \] Together, \(\|u\|^2\big(1-(\kappa-1)^{-2}\big)\le\kappa^2(2-\kappa)\): impossible if \(\kappa>2\).

The first line alone gives \(\kappa\le1+\sqrt{1-m_1}\); with \(\|\alpha\|\le1\), \(m_1\ge-1\), and that is \(1+\sqrt2\) again. \(m_1<0\) happens for some \(3\times3\) matrices. The second line needs \(E_n\) for every \(n\).

The canary

What the papers say the model did.

  • Jin. ChatGPT proposed sampling the Herglotz kernel at \(\lambda_i/2\) and adding the origin sample that cancels the correction. That is the compression in the transfer proof.
  • Lorist–Schwenninger. GPT-5.6, 25–26 July, asked for an improvement in estimates involving the iterates \(f^n\), pointed out that \(\Phi(f^{n+1})^*f(A)x-\|f(A)\|\Phi(f^n)^*x\) is controlled uniformly in \(n\) by the quantity behind \(m_1\). The lemma was abstracted from that. They saw Jin's preprint on 28 July.
  • Hartz. ChatGPT to discuss Jin's preprint.

The credited step is the same in both cases: stop estimating the companion of a single \(f\), and use the identity for all \(f^n\) simultaneously. The proofs were written and checked by the authors.

The completely bounded conjecture

Conjecture. For every \(m\ge1\) and every \(F=[f_{ij}]\in M_m(A(\Omega))\), \(\ \|F(T)\|\le2\sup_{z\in\Omega}\|F(z)\|\).

Equivalently \(f\mapsto f(T)\) has cb-norm \(\le2\); by Paulsen, \(T\) is then similar to an operator having \(\overline{W(T)}\) as a complete spectral set.

Known: \(1+\sqrt2\) (Crouzeix–Palencia); \(2\) for disks, and for weighted shifts (Crouzeix–Greenbaum 2025).

Neither Un nor Deux amplifies: \(M_m(A(\Omega))\) is not commutative, and both proofs rest on the companion commuting with \(f(T)\). Crouzeix–Palencia rest only on \(\|\alpha\|\le1\), which survives.
