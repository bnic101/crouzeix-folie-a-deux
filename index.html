Numerical range

Def. Let \(\mathcal H\) be a complex Hilbert space and \(T\in\mathcal B(\mathcal H)\) a bounded operator. The numerical range of \(T\) is \[ W(T)=\{\langle Tx,x\rangle:\ x\in\mathcal H,\ \|x\|=1\}. \]

In finite dimension closed and convex (Toeplitz–Hausdorff). In infinite dimension convex, \(\overline{W(T)}\supseteq\operatorname{conv}\sigma(T)\).

Spectral sets

Def. A set \(S\subseteq\mathbb C\) is called a \(K\)-spectral set for an operator \(T\in\mathcal B(\mathcal H)\) if \[ \|p(T)\|\le K\sup_{z\in S}|p(z)|\qquad\forall\,p\in\mathbb C[z]. \] (\(S\) bounded, polynomially convex.)

von Neumann's inequality. The disc is a spectral set for any contraction: \[ \|p(T)\|\le\sup_{z\in\mathbb D}|p(z)|,\qquad \|T\|\le1. \] (Also the closed half plane if \(\operatorname{Re}T=\tfrac{T+T^*}{2}\ge0\).)

Crouzeix conjecture

Conjecture (Crouzeix 2004). Let \(T\) be a bounded operator on a Hilbert space \(\mathcal H\). The numerical range \(\overline{W(T)}\) is a \(2\)-spectral set for \(T\).

Remark. This conjectured bound is sharp: \[ W\!\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\0&0\end{pmatrix}=D(0,\tfrac12),\qquad p(z)=z, \] \[ \Big\|\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\0&0\end{pmatrix}\Big\|=1=2\cdot\tfrac12=2\sup_{D(0,1/2)}|p(z)|. \]

Antecedents

  • von Neumann: constant \(1\), only for contractions.
  • Okubo–Ando 1975, Berger–Stampfli 1967: the disc is a \(2\)-spectral set.
  • Via \(2\)-dilations: \(W(T)\subset\overline{\mathbb D}\) iff \(T^n=2V^*U^nV\) (Berger).
  • Crouzeix 2004: true for \(2\times2\), equality only when \(W(T)\) is a disc.

Technique (all of the above): the double layer potential.

Double layer potential

\(\Omega\subset\mathbb C\) open bounded convex, smooth boundary, \(\overline{W(T)}\subset\Omega\). \(A(\Omega)\): holomorphic on \(\Omega\), continuous on \(\overline\Omega\). \(C(\partial\Omega)\): continuous on \(\partial\Omega\). Sup norms.

For \(\sigma\in\partial\Omega\) let \(\nu=\nu(\sigma)\) be the outward pointing normal; for \(z\in\Omega\) the double layer potential is \[ \mu(\sigma,z)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\Big(\underbrace{\frac{\nu}{\sigma-z}}_{\text{first layer}}+\underbrace{\frac{\bar\nu}{\bar\sigma-\bar z}}_{\text{second layer}}\Big)=\frac1\pi\operatorname{Re}\underbrace{\frac{\nu}{\sigma-z}}_{\text{Cauchy kernel}}. \]

Double layer potential

  • \(\mu\,ds=\tfrac1\pi\,d\arg(\sigma-z)\): the angle at which \(z\) sees \(d\sigma\).
  • \(\mu\ge0\): \(z\) inside every supporting line.
  • \(\int_{\partial\Omega}\mu\,ds=2\) for \(z\in\Omega\); \(1\) for \(z\in\partial\Omega\).

Why use? The Cauchy layer leads to a bound on \(f(z)\). If we can control the conjugate we win. (Adjoint when applying functional calculus.)

Double layer potential, cont.

Let \[ C(f,T)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\partial\Omega}f(\sigma)(\sigma-T)^{-1}\,d\sigma\in\mathcal B(\mathcal H) \] and \[ S(f,T)=C(f,T)+C(\bar f,T)^*\in\mathcal B(\mathcal H). \] By definition \(S\) is the function calculus integration against the double layer potential \(\mu(\sigma,z)\), i.e. \[ S(f,T)=\int_{\partial\Omega}f(\sigma)\,\mu(\sigma,T)\,ds, \] with \(\mu(\sigma,T)=\tfrac1\pi\operatorname{Re}[\nu(\sigma)(\sigma-T)^{-1}]\ge0\), \(\int\mu\,ds=2I\).

The question

Lemma (Crouzeix–Palencia 2017). For \(f\in C(\partial\Omega)\), \[ \|S(f,T)\|=\|f(T)+C(\bar f,T)^*\|\le2\|f\|_{\partial\Omega}. \]

Proof: \(\mu\ge0\), \(\int\mu\,ds=2I\), Cauchy–Schwarz.

So the question we must answer is: how does \(\|S(f,T)\|\) control \(\|f(T)\|\)? In particular, can we estimate \(C(\bar f,T)^*\) or make it vanish all together?

Positivity and the double layer formula

Consider \[ 2\Phi(f):=f(T)+C(\bar f,T)^*=f(T)+\alpha(f)(T)^*, \] where \[ \alpha(f)(z)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\partial\Omega}\frac{\overline{f(\sigma)}}{\sigma-z}\,d\sigma. \] Note that \(\alpha:A(\Omega)\to A(\Omega)\) is antilinear and \(\alpha(1)=1\).

Positivity of \(\Phi\) says \(2\|\Phi(f)\|\le2\|f\|_{\partial\Omega}\): the result from the previous slide, found in Crouzeix–Palencia.

Positivity and the double layer formula

Scalar case: \(2\Phi(f)(z)=f(z)+\overline{\alpha(f)(z)}\), holomorphic plus anti-holomorphic.

Disc: \(\alpha(f)=\overline{f(0)}\), a constant.

Crouzeix–Palencia: estimate the companion, \(\|\alpha(f)\|\le\|f\|\). Gives \(1+\sqrt2\).

An alternate approach (not taken by either ChatGPT proof) is to “hack” the formula by studying extremal functions: \(f\) extremal, \(\|f(T)\|\le\|2\Phi(f)\|\), which proves Crouzeix. (Bickel, Gorkin, Greenbaum, Overton.)

Equivalent formulation

Theorem (Twisted real part). Let \(\mathcal A\) be a unital commutative operator algebra, let \(\theta:\mathcal A\to\mathcal B(\mathcal H)\) be a bounded homomorphism and let \(\alpha:\mathcal A\to\mathcal A\) be a unital antilinear map such that the unital linear map \[ \theta_\alpha:\mathcal A\to\mathcal B(\mathcal H),\qquad a\mapsto\tfrac12\big(\theta(a)+\theta(\alpha(a))^*\big) \] is contractive. Then \(\|\theta\|\le2\).
  • Ransford–Schwenninger (2018): twisted real part ⇒ Crouzeix.
  • Michael Hartz (2026) proves the theorem using Jin's ChatGPT solution.

Function theory

Function theory

Theorem (Holomorphic → resolvent transfer; Jin, operator form Hartz). Let \(T\in\mathcal B(\mathcal H)\) with \(\sigma(T)\subset\overline{\mathbb D}\). Let \(H:\mathbb D\to\mathcal B(\mathcal H)\) be a holomorphic function such that \(H(0)=I\) and \(\operatorname{Re}H(z)\ge0\) for all \(z\in\mathbb D\). Assume that \[ H(z)-(I-zT)^{-1} \] commutes with \(T^*\) for all \(z\in\mathbb D\). Then \(\|T\|\le2\).

\(H(z)-(I-zT)^{-1}=0\): \(\operatorname{Re}(I-zT)^{-1}\ge0\) iff \(W(T)\subset\overline{\mathbb D}\) (Berger); conclusion is Okubo–Ando.

Transfer ⇒ twisted real part (Hartz)

Let \(a\in\mathcal A\), \(\|a\|\le1\), \(T=\theta(a)\). For \(z\in\mathbb D\) the Cayley transform \(c_z=(1+za)(1-za)^{-1}\) has \(\operatorname{Re}c_z\ge0\), and \(\theta_\alpha\) is positive, so \[ H(z):=\theta_\alpha(c_z) \] is holomorphic with \(H(0)=I\) and \(\operatorname{Re}H(z)\ge0\). Since \(\theta\) is a homomorphism, \(\theta(c_z)=2(I-zT)^{-1}-I\), hence \[ H(z)-(I-zT)^{-1}=\tfrac12\big(\theta(\alpha(c_z))^*-I\big), \] the adjoint of an element of \(\theta(\mathcal A)\), which is commutative and contains \(T\). So \(H(z)-(I-zT)^{-1}\) commutes with \(T^*\), and the transfer theorem gives \(\|\theta(a)\|\le2\).

Proof of the transfer theorem, I

Write \(R(z)=(I-zT)^{-1}\) and \(\Delta=H-R\). On the Hardy space \(H^2\otimes\mathcal H\) of \(\mathcal H\)-valued power series, \(\operatorname{Re}H\ge0\) gives \(\operatorname{Re}M_H\ge0\) for the multiplication operator, and \(M_H=M_R+M_\Delta\).

We want a subspace on which \(M_\Delta\) is invisible. For \(W\) with \(\sigma(W)\subset\mathbb D\) let \[ N_W=\{F=\textstyle\sum F_nz^n:\ \sum W^nF_n=0\}, \] the functions vanishing at \(W\), and \(\Gamma:\mathcal H\to N_W\) its reproducing kernel at \(0\).

  • If \(\Delta(z)\) commutes with \(W\), then \(M_\Delta N_W\subset N_W\).
  • Since \(\Delta(0)=0\), \(\Gamma^*M_\Delta\Gamma=0\).

Take \(W=\tfrac12T^*\). Then \[ \Gamma^*(M_R+M_R^*)\Gamma=\Gamma^*(M_H+M_H^*)\Gamma\ge0. \]

Proof of the transfer theorem, II

\(\Gamma\) can be written down: \(\Gamma=K_0-K_WP^{-1}\), with \(K_W\) the kernel map at \(W\) and \[ P=K_W^*K_W=\sum_{n\ge0}4^{-n}T^{*n}T^n,\qquad Q=K_W^*K_{T^*}=\sum_{n\ge0}2^{-n}T^{*n}T^n. \] The resolvent identity turns the inequality of the last slide into \[ 4Y-YP-PY\ge0,\qquad Y=Q-P\ge\tfrac14T^*T. \] If \(Px=\alpha x\) with \(\alpha>2\), testing on \(x\) gives \((4-2\alpha)\langle Yx,x\rangle\ge0\), so \(Yx=0\), so \(Tx=0\), so \(Px=x\). Hence \(P\le2I\). With \(P=I+\tfrac14T^*PT\), \[ T^*T\le T^*PT=4(P-I)\le4I. \]

Dilation theory

Dilation theory

Lemma (Lorist–Schwenninger, ChatGPT). Let \(\mathcal H\) be a Hilbert space and \(T\in\mathcal B(\mathcal H)\). Suppose there exists a Hilbert space \(\mathcal K\), a contraction \(Q\in\mathcal B(\mathcal K)\) and an isometry \(V\in\mathcal B(\mathcal H,\mathcal K)\) such that the operators \[ E_n:=2V^*Q^{*n}V-T^{*n},\qquad n\in\mathbb N, \] are uniformly bounded and commute with \(T\). Then \(\|T\|\le2\).

\(E_n=0\): \(2\)-dilation of \(T^*\), \(\|T\|\le2\).

Lemma ⇒ Crouzeix

Let \(\mathcal K=L^2(\partial\Omega,ds;\mathcal H)\) and \(Vx=\tfrac1{\sqrt2}\mu(\cdot,A)^{1/2}x\); \(V\) is an isometry because \(\int\mu\,ds=2I\). For \(\|f\|_\infty=1\) let \(Q\) be multiplication by \(f\), a contraction. Then \[ V^*Q^nV=\tfrac12\int f^n\mu\,ds=\Phi(f^n), \] and the identity for \(f^n\), adjointed, gives with \(T=f(A)\) \[ E_n=2\Phi(f^n)^*-f(A)^{*n}=\alpha(f^n)(A). \] \(E_n\) commutes with \(f(A)\) (both come from the functional calculus) and \(\|E_n\|\le\|\theta\|\|\alpha\|\). The lemma gives \(\|f(A)\|\le2\). The bound \(\|\alpha\|\le1\) is not used.

Proof of the lemma, I

Suppose \(\kappa=\|T\|>1\) and take a unit \(x\) with \(T^*Tx=\kappa^2x\) (ultrapower). Put \(m_n=\operatorname{Re}\langle E_nT^nx,x\rangle\) and \[ u=Q^*VTx-\kappa Vx, \] the failure of \(VTx\) to be a \(\kappa\)-eigenvector of \(Q^*\).

From the identity for \(n=1\), \(V\) isometric and \(Q\) contractive: \[ \|u\|^2\le 2\kappa^2-\kappa^3-\kappa m_1. \] This alone gives \(\kappa\le1+\sqrt{1-m_1}\); \(m_1\ge-1\) gives \(1+\sqrt2\), and \(m_1<0\) for some \(3\times3\) matrices.

Proof of the lemma, II

From the identities for all \(n\) and \(E_nT=TE_n\), completing a square, \[ \kappa m_n-m_{n+1}\ge-\frac{\|u\|^2}{\kappa(\kappa-1)}\qquad(n\ge1). \] Multiply by \(\kappa^{-n+1}\) and sum; the left side telescopes: \[ \kappa m_1\ge-\frac{\|u\|^2}{(\kappa-1)^2}. \] With the upper bound, \[ \|u\|^2\big(1-(\kappa-1)^{-2}\big)\le\kappa^2(2-\kappa), \] impossible for \(\kappa>2\).

Completely bounded

Conjecture. For every \(m\ge1\) and \(F=[f_{ij}]\in M_m(A(\Omega))\), \(\ \|F(T)\|\le2\sup_{z\in\Omega}\|F(z)\|\).
  • cb-norm \(\le2\); Paulsen: \(T\) similar to an operator with \(\overline{W(T)}\) a complete spectral set.
  • Known: \(1+\sqrt2\) (Crouzeix–Palencia); \(2\) for discs, weighted shifts (Crouzeix–Greenbaum 2025).
  • \(M_m(A(\Omega))\) not commutative: both new proofs need the companion to commute with \(f(T)\). Crouzeix–Palencia need only \(\|\alpha\|\le1\).
